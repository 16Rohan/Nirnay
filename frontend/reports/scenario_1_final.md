- **Trade-off**: Blue prioritized highway security over observation post density at LOC-BRAVO, accepting short-term vulnerability to maintain main supply route integrity.
- **Trade-off**: Red committed elite forces and specialized equipment to a high-risk crossing attempt, accepting potential attrition for strategic effect—now lost if not followed up.
- **Trade-off**: Both sides avoided escalation to kinetic cross-border fires, preserving diplomatic space but allowing Red to retain initiative for future attempts.
- **Uncertainty**: Red's true intent: whether the observed crossing attempt was a feint to draw Blue reserves or a genuine main effort now delayed.
- **Uncertainty**: The reliability of Blue's aerial reconnaissance in detecting low-signature Red engineer activities under vegetation or during low-visibility conditions.
- **Uncertainty**: Future river hydrology: whether discharge rates will continue to rise, fall, or stabilize, affecting feasibility of future crossings.
- **Strategic Implication**: Blue must improve persistent surveillance at chokepoints like LOC-BRAVO to prevent future undetected infiltrations, possibly through unattended ground sensors or increased patrol randomness.
- **Strategic Implication**: Red's demonstrated capability to organize and deploy complex river crossing operations suggests a need for Blue to pre-register artillery and mortar fire plans on likely crossing sites and prepare countermobility obstacles.
